## Reasoning

- Reasoning: Using facts and logic to arrive at an answer
	- Deductive Reasoning: Use logic to go from premise to firm conclusion
	- Inductive Reasoning: From observation, predict a likely conclusion
	- Abductive Reasoning: From observation, predict the most likely explanation
- Formal v.s. Informal
	- Formal Reasoning: Follows formal rules of logic along with axiomatic knowledge to derive conclusions.
	- Informal Reasoning: Uses intuition, experience, common sense to arrive at answers.
	- In the following context, by “reasoning” we mean informal deductive reasoning, often involving multiple steps

## Reasoning via Distillation

- Q. Can we get reasoning-like behavior with smaller LMs by teaching them to imitate larger models?
- **Orca**: Instruction-tuning small LMs with CoT Rationales
	1. Collect a wide variety of instructions from the FLAN-v2 collection
	2. Prompt GPT4 with these instructions along with a system message
	3. Finetune Llama-13b on outputs generated via GPT4
- **ReST<sup>EM</sup>**: Fine-tuning LMs on their own outputs
	- Alternates between the following two steps:
		1. Generate (E-Step): Given reasoning problem, sample multiple solutions from language model. Filter based on some (problem specific) function (answer correctness for math problems)
		2. Improve (M-Step): Update the language model to maximize probability of filtered solutions, using supervised fine-tuning
	- Note: Self-generated rationales can outperform human generated rationales.

## Evaluation

- Q. Can LM really reason? i.e. if reasoning in LMs is systematic?
- CoT rationales are often not faithful.
	- Not faithful = produce the correct answer without the full rationale, i.e. post-hoc explanations (事后解释)
	- One way is to force the model to early exist the rationale and produce a answer. A rationale consists of multiple sentences. 
	- Another way is to corrupt part of the rationale and then check whether the model generates the correct answer.

- Q. We wonder is repetition due to how Language Models (LMs) are trained? Is MLE always the choice for Model Learning?
	- Not really, MLE cannot handle sequential data well.
- Training with **teacher forcing** leads to **exposure bias** at generation time:
	- During training, the model’s inputs are *gold* context tokens from real, human-generated texts:
		$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MLE}} = -\log P(y_t^* | \{y^*\}_{<t})$
	- At generation time, the model’s inputs are previously-decoded tokens:
		$\mathcal{L}_{\text{dec}} = -\log P(\hat{y}_t | \{\hat{y}\}_{<t})$
- There are several solutions for exposure bias.
	- Scheduled sampling
		- With some probability p, decode a token and feed that as the next input, rather than the gold token
		- Increase *p* over the course of training
	- Dataset Aggregation (DAgger)
		- At various intervals during training, generate sequences from your current model
		- Add these sequences to your training set as additional examples
	- Retrieval Augmentation
		- Learn to retrieve a sequence from an existing corpus of human-written prototypes (e.g., dialogue responses)
		- Learn to edit the retrieved sequence by adding, removing, and modifying tokens in the prototype - this will still result in a more "human-like" generation
	- RL: cast your text generation model as a Markov decision process
		- State $s$ is the model's representation of the preceding context
		- Actions $a$ are the words that can be generated
		- Policy $\pi$ is the decoder
		- Rewards $r$ are provided by an external score
		- Backprop based on the reward
	- Note: For RL, the reward estimation is important!
		- We can not just use evaluation metrics, as they are merely proxies for generation quality.
		- Human Preference (RLHF: RL using Human Feedback) is the technique behind ChatGPT!
			- Human ranking the generated text based on their preference.
			- Learn a reward function of the human preference.
- Summary:
	- *Teacher forcing* is still the main algorithm for training text generation models
	- Exposure bias causes text generation models to lose coherence easily
		- Models must learn to recover from their own bad samples (e.g., scheduled sampling, DAgger)
		- Or not be allowed to generate bad text to begin with (e.g., retrieval + generation)
	- Training with RL can allow models to learn behaviors that are preferred by human preference/metrics.

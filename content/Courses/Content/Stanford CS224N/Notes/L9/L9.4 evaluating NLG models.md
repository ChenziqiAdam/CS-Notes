## Content Overlap Metrics

- Compute a score that indicates the lexical similarity between *generated* and *gold-standard (human-written)* text
	- Fast, efficient and widely used
	- N-gram overlap metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr)
- Bad for MT(machine translation), summarization, dialogue, story generation, and so on

## Model-Based Metrics

- Use *learned representations* of words and sentences to compute semantic similarity between generated and reference texts
	- No more n-gram bottleneck because text units are represented as **embeddings**!
	- The embeddings are pretrained, distance metrics used to measure the similarity can be fixed
- Vector Similarity
	- Word similarity
	- Sentence similarity

## Human Evaluation

- Q. How to evaluate an evaluation metric?
	- Use human evaluations as the gold standard
	- New automated metrics must correlate well with human evaluations
- Ask human to evaluate the quality of generated text from:
	- fluency
	- coherence/consistency
	- factuality and correctness
	- commonsense
	- style/formality
	- grammaticality
	- typicality
	- redundancy
- Issues with human evaluation
	- Slow and expensive
	- Not reproducible results (inconsistent)
	- Precision not recall
- Solution:
	- ADEM: A learned metric from human judgments for dialog system evaluation in a chatbot setting.
	- HUSE: Human Unified with Statistical Evaluation, determines the similarity of the output distribution and a human reference distribution.

## Summary

- Content overlap metrics provide a good starting point for evaluating the quality of generated text, but they're not good enough on their own.
- Model-based metrics can be more correlated with human judgment, but behavior is not interpretable
- Human judgments are critical
	- But humans are inconsistent!
- In many cases, the best judge of output quality is YOU!
	- Look at your model generations. Don't just rely on numbers!
	- Publicly release large samples of the output of systems that you create!

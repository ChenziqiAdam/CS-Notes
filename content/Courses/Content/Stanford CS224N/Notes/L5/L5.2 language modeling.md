- Language Modeling is the task of predicting what word comes next.
	- More formally: given a sequence of words $x^{(1)}, x^{(2)}, \ldots, x^{(t)}$, compute the probability distribution of the next word $x^{(t+1)}$:
		$P\left(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(1)}\right)$
		where $x^{(t+1)}$ can be any word in the vocabulary $V = \{w_1, \ldots, w_{|V|}\}$.
	- A system that does this is called a **Language Model**.
- You can also think of a **Language Model** as a system that **assigns a probability to a piece of text**
	- For example, if we have some text $x^{(1)}, \ldots, x^{(T)}$, then the probability of this text (according to the Language Model) is:
	$P(x^{(1)}, \ldots, x^{(T)}) = P(x^{(1)}) \times P(x^{(2)} \mid x^{(1)}) \times \cdots \times P(x^{(T)} \mid x^{(T-1)}, \ldots, x^{(1)})$
				 $= \prod_{t=1}^T P(x^{(t)} \mid x^{(t-1)}, \ldots, x^{(1)})$

# n-gram Language Models

- Definition: An n-gram is a chunk of n consecutive words.
	- unigrams: "the", "students", "opened", "their"
	- bigrams: "the students", "students opened", "opened their"
	- trigrams: "the students opened", "students opened their"
- Idea: Collect statistics about how frequent different n-grams are and use these to predict next word.
	- First, we make a **Markov assumption**: $x^{(t+1)}$ depends only on the preceding $n-1$ words
		$P(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(1)}) = P(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)})$  (assumption)
	- The probability of an $n$-gram can be expressed as:
		$P(x^{(t+1)}, x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)}) = P(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)}) \cdot P(x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)})$  (definition of conditional probability)
	- Q. How do we get these $n$-gram and $(n-1)$-gram probabilities?
		- By counting them in some large corpus of text!
			$P(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)}) \approx \frac{\text{count}(x^{(t+1)}, x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)})}{\text{count}(x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(t-n+2)})}$ (statistical approximation)
	- Note: there is no training! All is counting.

# Neural Language Model

- Recall the Language Modeling task:
	- **Input:** sequence of words $x^{(1)}, x^{(2)}, \ldots, x^{(t)}$
	- **Output:** probability distribution of the next word $P(x^{(t+1)} \mid x^{(t)}, \ldots, x^{(1)})$

- Summary
	- Improvements over n-gram LM:
		- No sparsity problem
		- Don't need to store all observed n-grams
	- Remaining problems:
		- Fixed window is too small
		- Enlarging window enlarges $W$
			- Window can never be large enough!
		- No symmetry in how the inputs are processed.
			- $x^{(1)}$ and $x^{(2)}$ are multiplied by completey different weights in $W$.
	- We need a neural architecture that can process *any length input*.

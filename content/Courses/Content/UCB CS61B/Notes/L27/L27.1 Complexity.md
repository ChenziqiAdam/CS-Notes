- Two approaches to manage complexity:
	- Making code simpler and more obvious
		- Eliminating special cases
		- Encapsulation into modules
			- In a modular design, creators of one "module" can use other modules without knowing how they work.
- **"Complexity is anything related to the structure of a software system that makes it hard to understand and modify the system."**
	Cost view of complexity
	- In a complex system, small improvements require big effort.
	- In a simple system, big improvements require small effort.
- Complexity also depends on how often a piece of a system is modified.
	- i.e. if some highly complex codes are rarely modified, the overall impact is minimal.
- Three symptoms of complexity
	1. **Change Amplification**: a simple change requires modification in many places.
	2. **Cognitive Load**: how much you need to know in order to make a change
	3. **Unknown Unknows**: you don't know what you need to make modifications
- In a good design, a system is ideally **obvious**. 
	In an **obvious** system, to make a change a developer can: 
	- Quickly understand how existing code works. 
	- Come up with a proposed change without doing too much thinking. 
	- Have a high confidence that the change should actually work, despite not reading much code
- Complexity is inevitable. But keep Zero Tolerance to bad code!
	- **Step back, refactor the code, and run your unit test!**

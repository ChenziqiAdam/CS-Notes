- We'll use the term ***rational*** in a very specific, technical way:
	- Rational: maximally achieving pre-defined goals
	- Goals are expressed in terms of the utility of outcomes
	- World is uncertain, so we'll use expected utility
	- Being rational means acting to maximize your expected utility
- Q. What is Intelligence?
	- Skill-based perspective
		- "A system is only intelligent if it can do ..."
			- Learn from experience?
			- Use words properly?
			- Make mistakes?
			- Not make mistakes?
	- Embodiment perspective
	- Psychometrics perspective
		- Measure abilities (broad range), not skills (abilities induce skill learning)
	- Human-compatible perspective
		- Machine's objective is to maximize human utility
		- Initially uncertain about human preferences
		- Must learn about preferences from human behavior
	- Brain can be a great inspiration for AI
		- Yet ~100T synapses in the human brain vs ~1.8T weights in GPT4

### Development of AI

- History of AI
	- 1940-1950: Neural and computer science meet
	- 1950-1970: Excitement! Logic-driven (perceptrons)
	- 1970-1990: knowledge-based approaches (knowledge system)
	- 1990-2010: statistical approaches, agents
	- 2010-2017: Big data, GPUs, Deep Learning
	- 2017-present: Scaling up, Large Language
		- 2017: Google invents Transformer architectures
		- 2022: OpenAI releases ChatGPT
- For now, cognitive and physical world problems are more difficult for AI to solve. 
	- One reason is that there is no data to train AI about how to move in reality.
	- Another one maybe that for physical problems, AI may not be generalized for all physical problems.
- Q. How to design rational agents?
	- An agent is an entity that perceives and acts.
	- A rational agent selects actions that maximize its (expected) utility.
	- Characteristics of the percepts, environment, and action space dictate techniques for selecting rational actions
